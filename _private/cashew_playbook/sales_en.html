01

Why buy this

Most published cashew research is one of two things: a market-sizing report built on growth rates, or promotional material from an origin government or trade body. Neither tells you what to verify before capital goes out the door.

This document was written to answer that one question. Three of its findings are disclosed in full below — enough to judge whether the rest is worth buying.

Disclosed 01

EUDR does not cover cashew

Annex I of the EU Deforestation Regulation lists cattle, cocoa, coffee, oil palm, rubber, soya and wood. Cashew appears on none of them. Application itself has also been deferred to 30 December 2026 for large operators.

Numerous cashew feasibility studies nonetheless carry EUDR compliance in the budget, having imported the assumption from cocoa or coffee. This single line removes months of compliance scoping and its cost.

Disclosed 02

The US tariff on Vietnamese product is 20%, not 46%

The 46% figure was a negotiating scenario discussed in the first half of 2025 and never implemented. The operative frame, published by USTR in October 2025, is a 20% reciprocal tariff.

A model still running 46% materially overstates the case for relocating away from Vietnam. A feasibility study built on that number will not survive diligence.

Disclosed 03

2025 was a record revenue year and margins narrowed

Vietnamese kernel exports reached US$5.23bn in 2025, an all-time high. Over the same period kernel realisations rose 14.0% while raw material cost per tonne of kernel rose 18.2%.

The report works that spread through on a per-tonne basis and shows the outturn level at which it turns negative. Method and assumptions are stated in full, so the calculation can be rerun on your own cost base.

Why give these away

All three are facts, not analysis. Disclosing them costs the report nothing and demonstrates that the remaining eight chapters were held to the same standard. The fastest way to judge research is to check whether it corrects a widely held error.

03

How this differs

Every claim carries a confidence rating

A reproducible from a named institutional source · B sources disagree, range shown · C uncorroborated industry estimate

On the judgement that an honest range serves a decision better than a confident single number.

Disagreement is disclosed, not resolved

Published CNSL market estimates for 2025 span US$0.51bn to US$6.96bn — a thirteenfold spread. The report shows all three and states plainly that none of them should be used to underwrite an investment.

A report that quotes the largest figure is not doing diligence on your behalf.

A data trap was caught and excluded

FAOSTAT's 2024 trade data is incomplete. Vietnamese RCN imports appear at 1.42 Mt against 2.65 Mt in customs reporting. Plotted as-is it produces a striking and entirely false "Vietnam collapse" conclusion.

Production and trade domains were assessed separately for completeness, and the reasoning is stated in the document.

Republished errors were traced

Several widely cited market-research figures attribute FAOSTAT 2023 values to 2024 — India at 782,000 t and Vietnam at 347,634 t among them.

Caught because the underlying data was read directly rather than cited second-hand.
